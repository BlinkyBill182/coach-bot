# Exercise reference

Evidence-based notes for choosing exercises and giving technique cues. Studies were checked against their PubMed or journal abstracts (October 2026). "Practical" marks coaching judgement with no direct trial behind it. Hebrew names match the exercise library (config/exercises).

Priorities first: calories and consistency drive lean gain; exercise choice improves results per set. A perfect exercise list does nothing in a calorie deficit or with missed sessions.

## Principles

**Volume.** More weekly sets per muscle give more growth, with diminishing returns.
- Schoenfeld, Ogborn, Krieger 2017 (J Sports Sci; meta-regression, 15 studies): each extra weekly set added about 0.37% growth; higher vs lower volume within studies was about 3.9% more.
- Pelland et al. 2026 (Sports Med; 67 studies, 2,058 people): volume helped hypertrophy with diminishing returns. Counting an indirect set as half a set fit best (rows count half for biceps, presses half for triceps and front delts). Frequency mattered little for hypertrophy once volume was equal.
- Use: add sets to a lagging muscle before adding exercises. The leg targets in SKILL.md (about 10 quads, 9 hamstrings, 8 calves per week) stand.

**Proximity to failure.** Closer to failure grows more, but going all the way adds little.
- Robinson et al. 2024 (Sports Med; meta-regression): hypertrophy rose as sets ended closer to failure; strength did not depend on it.
- Refalo et al. 2023 (Sports Med; 15 studies): failure vs non-failure was trivial (ES 0.12, not significant).
- Use: 1-2 reps in reserve on compounds; the last set of an isolation exercise to failure (as SKILL.md says). If the user says "could have done more", the load is too light.

**Rest.** Longer rest helps up to a point.
- Schoenfeld et al. 2016 (J Strength Cond Res; 21 trained men, 8 weeks): 3 min rest gave more strength and more thigh thickness than 1 min.
- Singer et al. 2024 (Front Sports Act Living; meta-analysis, 9 studies): a small benefit from resting more than 60 s, no appreciable difference beyond 90 s.
- Use: at least 90 s between sets for the same muscle; 2-3 min on heavy compounds. In supersets the other exercises count toward that rest. If reps drop sharply by set 3 (for example chest press 55×8 then 55×5), the cause is usually rest, not load.

**Rep ranges.** Light and heavy loads grow muscle about equally when sets go near failure; heavy loads build more strength.
- Schoenfeld et al. 2017 (J Strength Cond Res; 21 studies): similar hypertrophy, more 1RM gain with loads over 60% 1RM.
- Lopez et al. 2021 (Med Sci Sports Exerc; 28 studies): no hypertrophy difference between ≤8RM, 9-15RM and >15RM.
- Use: 6-12 reps on compounds is efficient. 10-20 on small isolation lifts (lateral raise, calves, abs), where heavy loads mostly add joint stress.

**Progression.** Adding reps works as well as adding load.
- Plotkin et al. 2022 (PeerJ; 43 trained people, 8 weeks): both methods gave similar growth and strength.
- Use: the double progression in SKILL.md (reps to the top of the range, then add load) is supported.

**Exercise order.** Order matters for strength, not size.
- Nunes et al. 2021 (Eur J Sport Sci; 11 studies): hypertrophy did not differ by order; strength gained more in whichever exercise came first.
- Use: put the lift the user most wants to progress first. On leg days the order can stay flexible; whatever comes second comes in lighter, which is expected.

**Long muscle lengths (the stretch).** Growth is usually equal or greater when the muscle works in its stretched position.
- Kassiano et al. 2023 (J Strength Cond Res; review, 11 studies): full ROM, or partials in the stretched part of the range, beat partials in the short (contracted) part for several muscles.
- Supporting trials: Maeo 2021 (hamstrings), Maeo 2023 (triceps), Pedrosa 2022 (quads), Kinoshita 2023 and Kassiano 2023 (calves), Sato 2021 and Pedrosa 2023 (biceps). Details are under each exercise.
- Counterpoint: Varovic et al. 2025 (Int J Sports Med; 12 studies) found trivial differences overall. Wolf et al. 2023 (Int J Strength Cond) found full ROM and partials close, with a possible but uncertain edge for long-length partials. Wolf et al. 2025 (PeerJ; 30 trained people, 8 weeks) found lengthened partials equal to full ROM for arm thickness.
- Use: never cut the bottom of a rep. Take the deep stretch under control. When the user is near failure, a few extra partial reps in the stretched half are fine. Prefer exercise versions that load the stretch (seated leg curl, overhead triceps, straight-knee calf raise), but don't promise large differences.

**Mind-muscle focus.** It helps on isolation lifts.
- Schoenfeld et al. 2018 (Eur J Sport Sci; 30 untrained men, 8 weeks): focusing on squeezing the muscle gave more biceps growth (12.4% vs 6.9%). The quads showed no difference.
- Use: cue the muscle on curls, raises and flyes ("feel the side delt lift the weight"). On presses and leg press, cue the movement.

**Spot reduction does not work.** Visible abs come from body fat.
- Vispute et al. 2011 (J Strength Cond Res; 24 adults, 6 weeks of daily ab work): no change in abdominal fat or waist size.
- Use: abs show when body fat is low enough. The weekly weight rule in SKILL.md is the fat guard; ab exercises only build the muscle underneath.

**When to swap an exercise.** Practical rules:
1. Stalled 3 sessions in a row: same load, no added reps, with sleep and food on target. Check rest and form first, then swap to an alternative listed below.
2. Joint pain (not muscle burn) that repeats on the exercise: swap now. Don't push through.
3. Poor feel in the target muscle after 2-3 sessions of trying the cues: swap.
- Keep one swap at a time, so the effect is readable. Plan changes need the user's yes.

## Program design (split, frequency, weekly sets)

**What the research says:**
- **The split hardly matters when weekly sets are equal.** Ramos-Campo et al. 2024 (J Strength Cond Res; 14 studies, 392 people): split and full-body training gave the same growth in the arms, the thigh and lean mass. No RCT compares push/pull/legs with upper/lower directly.
- **Frequency matters little once volume is equal.**
  - Schoenfeld, Grgic, Krieger 2019 (J Sports Sci; 25 studies): no significant difference, including in trained lifters. This replaced the 2016 "at least twice a week" conclusion.
  - Pelland 2026 (Sports Med): frequency helps strength, while its effect on size is compatible with negligible.
  - Brigatto 2019 (J Strength Cond Res; 20 trained men): 16 sets once a week vs 8 sets twice a week gave the same growth.
  - Practical: twice a week makes it easier to fit 10-20 sets without very long sessions.
- **Weekly sets per muscle are the main lever, with diminishing returns.**
  - Schoenfeld 2017 and Pelland 2026 (both above).
  - Schoenfeld et al. 2019 (Med Sci Sports Exerc; 34 trained men, 8 weeks): 5 sets per exercise grew the arms and thighs more than 1 set. Elbow extensors grew +5.5% vs +1.1%.
  - Baz-Valle et al. 2022 (J Hum Kinet; trained adults): 12-20 sets a week is a sound range. Above 20 helped the triceps but not the biceps or quads.
- **Per session:** an unreviewed preprint (Remmert et al. 2025) finds no detectable extra growth beyond about 11 sets per muscle in one session.
- **Arms need direct work too.**
  - Pelland 2026: counting a press or row as half a set fits the data best.
  - Brandão et al. 2020 (J Strength Cond Res; 43 untrained men, 10 weeks): bench press alone grew the triceps long head +2.1%, vs +17.5% with an elbow-extension exercise.
  - Mannarino et al. 2021: curls grew the elbow flexors more than rows (+11% vs +5%).
  - In trained men, adding arm isolation to compounds gave no significant difference in arm circumference (+3.2% vs +1.3%, de França et al. 2015). That's the weakest part of the case for direct arm work.

**Coaches' positions** (from their own material; summarised, not quoted):
- Helms (The Muscle and Strength Pyramid), RP / Israetel, Nuckols (Stronger by Science), Ethier (Built With Science) and Nippard all land on the same core:
  - about 10-20 hard sets per muscle per week;
  - at least 2 sessions per muscle;
  - at most about 10-12 sets per muscle in one session;
  - at least one isolation exercise each for the triceps and biceps.
- Nippard and Ethier both use Push / Pull / Legs / Upper / Lower as a 5-day layout. Ethier calls it their personal favourite.
- They disagree on the top end. Helms, RP and Nuckols allow 20-30 sets for a priority muscle; Ethier gives 15-30. A once-a-week body-part split works only at modest volume (Helms, RP).

**The week** (direct sets per week; see SKILL.md for the order):
- Chest 12, back 12, side delts 9, rear delts 3.
- Triceps 9 (about 15 with presses at half), biceps 9 (about 15 with pulls at half).
- Quads 12, hamstrings 9, calves 9, abs 9.

**Next lever:** if the arms lag after 8 weeks, add 3 sets each (to about 18-20 counted sets) before changing the split.

## Exercises

Each entry: target, why this one, setup, cues, mistakes. For the load: start with a weight that allows the top of the rep range with control, then follow the double progression in SKILL.md and the user's logged numbers.

### לחיצת חזה (chest press, machine)
- **Target:** chest, mainly the sternal (mid and lower) head; front delts and triceps assist.
- **Why:** a machine is stable, so sets can go close to failure safely without a spotter. Rodríguez-Ridao et al. 2020 (IJERPH; EMG only, 30 trained adults): a flat press gave the highest mid and lower pec activity.
- **Setup:** set the seat so the handles line up with mid-chest. Shoulder blades back and down, feet flat.
- **Cues:**
  - Let the handles come back until the chest stretches, about in line with the torso.
  - Press without shrugging; keep the shoulder blades pinned.
  - 1-2 s on the way back, no bounce.
- **Mistakes:** short reps that stop well before the stretch; shoulders rolling forward at the end; the seat too high, which moves the work to the front delts.
- **Alternatives:** dumbbell flat press (more stretch at the bottom); Smith machine flat press.

### לחיצת חזה עליון (incline chest press)
- **Target:** chest, clavicular (upper) head; front delts.
- **Why:** EMG shows upper-pec activity peaks at about 30° incline. Above 45° the front delt takes over (Rodríguez-Ridao 2020, EMG only).
- **Setup:** bench or seat at about 30°, not steeper. Handles at upper-chest level.
- **Cues:**
  - Elbows about 45-60° from the torso, not flared to 90°.
  - Full stretch at the bottom, then press up and slightly in.
  - Keep the chest up; don't let the shoulders roll forward.
- **Mistakes:** a bench too steep, which turns it into a shoulder press; cutting the bottom of the rep; heavy load right after flat pressing, with no rest.
- **Alternatives:** incline dumbbell press at 30°; low-to-high cable fly as a second upper-chest movement.

### פרפר או קרוס אובר (fly, pec deck or cable)
- **Target:** chest, through shoulder horizontal adduction, with less triceps.
- **Why:** Pinto et al. 2025 (J Strength Cond Res; 15 untrained men, 12 weeks): pec deck alone grew both pec heads about 17-21%. No trial compares fly with press head to head. It is a useful third chest exercise when presses are limited by the triceps or shoulders.
- **Setup:** handles at chest height, slight elbow bend that stays fixed.
- **Cues:**
  - Open until the chest stretches, then hug a wide tree.
  - Squeeze for 1 s with the hands together.
- **Mistakes:** bending and straightening the elbows (that turns it into a press); going so heavy the stretch gets cut.
- **Alternatives:** cable crossover (mid or low-to-high).

### הרחקה לצדדים (lateral raise)
- **Target:** side (lateral) delt, which builds shoulder width.
- **Why:** dumbbell and cable gave the same growth (Larsen et al. 2025, Front Physiol; 24 trained adults, 8 weeks, about 3-5% thickness gain either way). Pick whichever feels better on the shoulder.
- **Setup:** stand tall, slight forward lean, arms a little in front of the body.
- **Cues:**
  - Lead with the elbows, out not up, to about shoulder height.
  - Lower slowly, about 2 s.
  - Thumbs level or slightly down is fine; stop at shoulder height.
- **Mistakes:** swinging from the hips; shrugging (the traps take the load); load so heavy the range shrinks.
- **Alternatives:** cable lateral raise behind the body; machine lateral raise.

### הרמה קדמית (front raise)
- **Target:** front delt.
- **Why:** the front delt already works hard in every press (Rodríguez-Ridao 2020 EMG; Pelland 2026 counts presses as half sets for it). On push and upper days front raise adds the least of any exercise in the plan. Practical: it is the first exercise to cut when a session runs long (approved).
- **Setup:** stand tall, dumbbells or a cable in front of the thighs.
- **Cues:**
  - Raise to eye level, no higher.
  - Lower under control; no hip swing.
- **Mistakes:** leaning back to swing the weight; lifting above the head.
- **Alternatives:** none needed while two presses are in the plan.

### הרחקה אחורית בישיבה כפופה (rear delt fly, bent-over seated)
- **Target:** rear (posterior) delt, upper back.
- **Why:** it balances heavy pressing. Schoenfeld et al. 2013 (J Strength Cond Res; EMG only, 19 trained men): a neutral grip (palms facing each other) gave more rear-delt activity than palms down. No hypertrophy trial compares rear-delt exercises.
- **Setup:** sit at the end of a bench, chest close to the thighs, back flat. Neutral grip.
- **Cues:**
  - Arms wide, pinkies leading, slight elbow bend.
  - Think "hands out to the walls", not "squeeze the shoulder blades".
  - Pause at the top, lower in 2 s.
- **Mistakes:** too heavy, so the traps and rhomboids row the weight up; a rounded back.
- **Alternatives:** reverse pec deck with a neutral grip; cable reverse fly.

### כפיפות צד (side bends, 24 kg kettlebell, reps per side)
- **Target:** obliques and quadratus lumborum.
- **Evidence:** no study measured oblique growth or waist width from side bends. Everything here is practical.
- **Setup:** stand tall, kettlebell in one hand, other hand behind the head or on the hip.
- **Cues:**
  - Bend straight to the side, not forward. Stretch, then pull up with the opposite side.
  - Slow on the way down, 1 s pause at the bottom.
- **Mistakes:** twisting or bending forward; bouncing out of the bottom; using both sides in alternation, which halves the rest.
- **Progress:** past the heaviest kettlebell in the gym, use 3 s down, a 2 s pause at the bottom, or a cable side bend, where the stack goes higher.
- **Alternatives:** cable side bend; cable crunch for the front abs (rectus abdominis).

### פולי עליון (lat pulldown)
- **Target:** lats, with the biceps and rear delts assisting.
- **Why:** it is easy to load and progress. Lusk et al. 2010 (J Strength Cond Res; EMG only, 12 men): a pronated (palms forward) grip gave more lat activity than palms facing you; grip width made no difference. No hypertrophy trial compares pulldown grips, or pulldown with row.
- **Setup:** thighs locked under the pad, grip a little wider than the shoulders, palms forward.
- **Cues:**
  - Start each rep with a full stretch: arms straight, shoulders up.
  - Pull the elbows down to the ribs; the hands just hold on.
  - Lean back about 10-15°, no swinging.
- **Mistakes:** jerking the torso back; stopping short of the stretch; pulling behind the neck.
- **Alternatives:** single-arm cable pulldown (more stretch per side); assisted pull-up.

### חתירה במכונה (machine row)
- **Target:** mid back (rhomboids, middle traps) and lats; rear delts and biceps assist.
- **Why:** chest-supported, so the low back is spared and every rep can go near failure.
- **Setup:** chest on the pad, handles at a full arm's reach.
- **Cues:**
  - Let the shoulder blades travel forward at the start (the stretch).
  - Drive the elbows back past the torso, then squeeze for 1 s.
  - Keep the chest on the pad.
- **Mistakes:** leaning back off the pad to move more weight; short reps without the forward stretch.
- **Alternatives:** seated cable row; chest-supported T-bar row (already in the library).

### יד קדמית (biceps cable curl)
- **Target:** biceps (elbow flexors).
- **Why:** curls that load the stretched, arm-straight part of the range grew more. Sato et al. 2021 (Front Physiol; 32 untrained adults, 5 weeks): training at 0-50° of elbow flexion gave +8.9% thickness vs +3.4% at 80-130°. Pedrosa et al. 2023 (Sports; 19 untrained women, 8 weeks, preacher curl): the long-length arm grew more in the distal biceps.
- **Setup:** cable at the bottom, step back so the cable pulls the arm slightly behind the body at the start.
- **Cues:**
  - Fully straighten the arm at the bottom every rep.
  - Elbow stays fixed at the side; curl without the shoulder coming forward.
  - Squeeze for 1 s, lower in 2-3 s.
- **Mistakes:** half reps that never reach a straight arm; swinging the torso; the elbow drifting forward.
- **Alternatives:** incline dumbbell curl (arms behind the body, big stretch); preacher curl with a full bottom range.

### יד אחורית (triceps: pushdown and overhead)
- **Target:** triceps. The long head is the largest; it crosses the shoulder and is stretched only when the arm is overhead.
- **Why:** Maeo et al. 2023 (Eur J Sport Sci; 21 adults, 12 weeks): overhead cable extension grew the whole triceps about 1.4 times more than an arms-at-the-side extension (long head +28.5% vs +19.6%), with 34-39% lighter loads.
- **Pushdown setup:** cable high, elbows pinned at the sides.
  - Cues: straighten fully and squeeze; let the hands rise to about chest height before the next rep.
- **Overhead setup:** cable low or mid, face away, rope or bar behind the head, elbows pointing up.
  - Cues: lower until the triceps stretches, elbows staying narrow; extend fully overhead.
- **Mistakes:** the elbows flaring or moving forward (the chest and shoulders take over); cutting the stretch on overhead work.
- **Alternatives:** single-arm overhead cable extension; dumbbell overhead extension.

### כפיפות בטן בכבל (cable crunch)
- **Target:** rectus abdominis (the six-pack).
- **Evidence:** no peer-reviewed trial measured rectus abdominis growth from cable crunches. The choice is practical: the cable gives a load that can progress past bodyweight, through a full spinal flexion range.
- **Setup:** kneel facing a high cable with a rope, hands by the forehead, hips still.
- **Cues:**
  - Curl the ribs toward the pelvis, rounding the spine. Don't hinge at the hips.
  - Exhale hard at the bottom and hold for 1 s.
  - Return to a long, stretched torso before the next rep.
- **Mistakes:** sitting back on the heels and pulling with the arms or hips; going so heavy the spine stops bending.
- **Alternatives:** machine crunch; decline crunch holding a plate.

### פשיטת ברכיים (leg extension)
- **Target:** quadriceps. It is the only quad exercise that trains the rectus femoris well, since that muscle also crosses the hip.
- **Why:**
  - Pedrosa et al. 2022 (Eur J Sport Sci; 45 untrained women): training the deep-bend half of the range (100-65° knee flexion) grew the quads more than the straight-leg half or full range.
  - Larsen et al. 2025 (J Sports Sci; 22 untrained men, 10 weeks): a reclined seat (hip at about 40°) grew the rectus femoris more than an upright seat; the vastus lateralis did not differ.
- **Setup:** knee joint lined up with the machine axis; pad just above the ankle. Recline the backrest if the machine allows.
- **Cues:**
  - Start from the deepest knee bend the machine gives.
  - Extend fully and hold for 1 s.
  - Lower in 2-3 s; don't let the stack touch.
- **Mistakes:** kicking the weight up and dropping it; cutting the bottom of the range; the hips lifting off the seat.
- **Alternatives:** single-leg extension; reverse Nordic (bodyweight, advanced).

### דדליפט רומני (Romanian deadlift, dumbbells)
- **Target:** hamstrings and glutes, trained by a hip hinge at long muscle length. It complements the leg curl, which trains knee flexion.
- **Why:** practical. It is the hamstring exercise a plan with only a leg curl lacks: the leg curl was the only one, at 6 sets a cycle. Training the hamstrings stretched is in line with Maeo 2021 (seated curl).
- **Setup:** dumbbells in front of the thighs, feet hip-width, knees slightly bent and fixed.
- **Cues:**
  - Push the hips back, dumbbells close to the legs, back flat.
  - Lower until the hamstrings stretch, usually just below the knees. Stop before the back rounds.
  - Drive the hips forward to stand. Don't lean back at the top.
- **Mistakes:** rounding the lower back; bending the knees more and turning it into a squat; going lower than the hamstrings allow.
- **Alternatives:** back extension or 45° hip extension; a second set of seated leg curl.

### כפיפת מרפקים בשיפוע (incline dumbbell curl)
- **Target:** biceps, especially the long head, with the arm behind the body (a stretched position).
- **Why:** curls at long muscle length grew more (Sato 2021, Pedrosa 2023, see יד קדמית). It adds a second biceps exercise.
- **Setup:** bench at about 45-60°, arms hanging straight down behind the torso, palms forward.
- **Cues:**
  - Keep the upper arm still and pointing at the floor; curl without the elbow drifting forward.
  - Lower all the way to a straight arm in 2-3 s.
- **Mistakes:** too heavy, so the shoulders roll forward; half reps at the top.
- **Alternatives:** Bayesian cable curl (cable behind, facing away); preacher curl with a full bottom range.

### כפיפת ברכיים (leg curl: seated vs lying)
- **Target:** hamstrings.
- **Why seated:** Maeo et al. 2021 (Med Sci Sports Exerc; 20 adults, 12 weeks, MRI): the seated leg curl grew the hamstrings +14% vs +9% for the lying (prone) curl. Sitting with the hips bent stretches the hamstrings.
- **Setup (seated):** knee joint lined up with the axis, thigh pad tight, lean slightly forward to stretch more.
- **Cues:**
  - Start with the legs fully straight (the stretch).
  - Curl all the way under the seat, squeeze for 1 s.
  - Lower in 3 s.
- **Mistakes:** a loose thigh pad, so the hips rise; short reps at the top only.
- **Open question:** which machine the user uses (seated or lying) is not logged yet. Ask once and save the answer with remember.
- **Alternatives:** lying leg curl when no seated machine is free; Romanian deadlift (hip hinge, trains the hamstrings at long length).

### דחיקת רגליים (leg press)
- **Target:** quadriceps; glutes and adductors more with deeper reps.
- **Why:**
  - Larsen et al. 2025 (J Sports Sci; 23 trained adults, 8 weeks): going to about 100° knee bend grew the quads as much as going to full depth (about 154°).
  - Kubo et al. 2019 (Eur J Appl Physiol; squat, 17 men, 10 weeks): full-depth squats grew the glutes and adductors more than half squats; the quads were similar.
  - Use: go as deep as the low back stays on the pad.
- **Setup:** feet shoulder-width, mid-platform. Low back and hips pressed into the seat.
- **Cues:**
  - Lower until the knees reach at least 90°, deeper if the hips stay down.
  - Knees track over the toes.
  - Don't lock the knees at the top.
- **Mistakes:** the hips rolling up off the pad at the bottom (low-back strain); bouncing out of the bottom; quarter reps with a huge load.
- **Alternatives:** hack squat; Smith machine squat.

### תאומים בישיבה (seated calf raise)
- **Target:** soleus. A bent knee slackens the gastrocnemius.
- **Why it's limited:** Kinoshita et al. 2023 (Front Physiol; 14 untrained adults, 12 weeks, MRI): seated raises barely grew the gastrocnemius (+0.6-1.7%), and the soleus grew no more than with standing raises (+2.9% vs +2.1%). The standing leg grew the whole calf more (+5.6% vs +2.1%).
- **Setup:** pad on the lower thighs, balls of the feet on the edge.
- **Cues:**
  - Drop the heels as low as possible and pause for 2 s in the stretch.
  - Rise fully onto the toes; no bouncing.
- **Mistakes:** bouncing in the bottom (the tendon does the work); half reps.

### תאומים בעמידה (straight-knee calf raise, standing machine or leg press)
- **Target:** gastrocnemius, the visible upper calf, plus the soleus.
- **Why:** Kinoshita 2023 above: straight-knee raises grew the gastrocnemius about 9-12% vs about 1-2% seated. Kassiano et al. 2023 (J Strength Cond Res; 42 women, 8 weeks): reps in the stretched part of the range (heel below the step to neutral) grew the medial gastrocnemius +15.2% vs +6.7% for full range.
- **Setup:** standing calf machine, or feet low on the leg press platform with the knees straight but not locked.
- **Cues:**
  - Deep stretch at the bottom with a 2 s pause.
  - Rise fully.
  - In the last reps, partials in the bottom half are fine.
